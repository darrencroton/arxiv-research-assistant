# Arxiv Priorities

## Categories
- astro-ph.GA
- astro-ph.CO

## Priorities - Science
1. Galaxy formation and evolution at all epochs. Strong fits directly explain galaxy stellar mass assembly, star formation histories, morphology, quenching, feedback, or cosmic star formation, especially JWST-era early galaxies, cosmic dawn/noon, and population-level local galaxy evolution. Do not strongly promote Milky Way structure, individual stars, isolated ISM chemistry, or generic dust papers unless the central result constrains galaxy evolution.
2. Black holes and AGN in the context of galaxy evolution. Strong fits connect SMBH growth, AGN triggering, feedback, demographics, duty cycles, obscuration, LRDs, or high-z/JWST AGN to host-galaxy evolution or galaxy populations. Do not strongly promote AGN variability, accretion physics, BLR structure, or black-hole phenomenology unless the paper connects it to galaxy evolution, demographics, feedback, or co-evolution.
3. The galaxy-halo connection. Strong fits connect galaxies to dark matter haloes through SHMR, abundance matching, SHAM, HOD, galaxy bias, assembly bias, clustering, halo mass inference, satellite/central occupation, or merger histories. Do not strongly promote general cosmology, dark matter theory, or large-scale-structure methods unless galaxies and their halo connection are central.
4. Galaxy environments and their effects on galaxy evolution. Strong fits address groups, clusters, filaments, voids, cosmic web, environmental quenching, ram pressure stripping, tidal effects, pre-processing, galaxy interactions, mergers, satellites, or close pairs as drivers of galaxy evolution. Do not strongly promote environment catalogues or cluster physics unless they explain galaxy properties or evolution.
5. Intracluster and intragroup light. Strong fits address ICL, IGL, diffuse stellar envelopes, stellar populations in diffuse light, or diffuse light as a tracer of group/cluster assembly.

## Priorities - Methods
1. AI and LLMs used for scientific discovery or data exploration in astronomy. Strong fits use AI/LLMs as part of the scientific method. Generic machine learning, neural networks, or transformers are only strong fits when paired with AI/LLM-style reasoning or when they directly advance the science priorities above.
2. Semi-analytic and semi-empirical galaxy models. Strong fits include SAM predictions and comparisons with data (SAGE, L-Galaxies, SHARK, Santa Cruz SAM, GALFORM, and similar), abundance matching, SHAM, stellar-to-halo mass modelling, HOD modelling, or galaxy-halo inference tied to the science priorities.
3. Cosmological N-body and hydrodynamical simulations. Strong fits use large-volume or zoom simulations, merger trees, subhalo catalogues, synthetic observations, or mock catalogues to address the science priorities above. Simulation-method papers are weaker unless the science target is galaxy formation, AGN/SMBH co-evolution, halo connection, environments, or ICL. Simulation products are strong method matches when they deliver calibrated galaxy luminosity functions, mock catalogues, clustering measurements, or model benchmarks used to test galaxy evolution.
4. Galaxy surveys as benchmarks or tests for theory. Strong fits use deep/wide photometric surveys, spectroscopic surveys, IFU surveys, or multiwavelength surveys to motivate, calibrate, validate, or challenge models of galaxy formation and evolution. Survey catalogues alone are weaker unless they deliver a clear galaxy-evolution or model-benchmark result.
5. Statistical tools for galaxy clustering and large-scale structure. Strong fits use 2PCF, projected/angular correlations, power spectra, cross-correlations, marked statistics, covariance modelling, halo occupation analysis, or bias measurements to probe galaxy-halo connection, environment, or galaxy evolution. Generic cosmological statistics are weaker unless the galaxy connection is central.
